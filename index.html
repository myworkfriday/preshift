SERVICE — THE FOUR ACTIONS (EVERY SHIFT)

Service is the actions — what you do. Hospitality is the feeling — how the guest feels while you do it, and that's different for every guest. Teach the actions; the feeling is the read.

  • Greet & Guide — 60 seconds. Name, welcome, "when was the last time you were here?", and a beverage recommendation that starts the meal right. You take control of the table's start.
  • Order & Confirm — guide, don't just write: "the short rib is the move tonight." Confirm temps, sides, mods, allergies. Repeat it back.
  • Deliver & Maintain — food to the right seat, never auction. Refills, clear empties, "your Brussels are still coming." Keep the table moving.
  • Check & Close — back in 2 minutes or 2 bites. Dessert before the check. Close it warm — you end the meal, you don't just drop a bill.

Why Guest recovery

No restaurant is perfect — what sets us apart is how we respond when something goes wrong. A recovered guest leaves more loyal than one who never had a problem. LEARN is the script.

The how, in one line each:

  • L — Listen. Let them fully explain. Don't interrupt. Nod, eye contact, stay calm.
  • E — Empathize. "I completely understand why that's frustrating."
  • A — Apologize. Take ownership, don't deflect. "I'm so sorry we missed the mark."
  • R — Resolve. Fix it fast. Get management involved immediately when needed.
  • N — Notify. Tell the manager and the kitchen what happened — it's how we get better.

Dairy — Most-flagged allergen on the sheet — Bleu Cheese Chips, Cheese Curds, Spinach Parm Dip, Tomato Bisque, Mac & Cheese, mashed potatoes, Bread Pudding, Oreo Cheesecake, and dozens more.

Walk it with the 4 R's:

  1. 1. Refer — manager and chef on duty, immediately. Never alone on this one.
  2. 2. Review — the guide, the ingredients, the prep. Cross-contact is real: same fryer, same board, same garnish.
  3. 3. Remember — never assume anything is safe. If you don't know, you don't serve until you do.
  4. 4. Respond — confirm with the guest in plain words what you did.

And the MUST rules from the allergy sheet: hit the Allergy button in the POS with the specific allergy, fill out a red Allergy Alert ticket for expo, and the manager or chef signs off.

WEEKLY STORY (READ-ALOUD)

Danny Meyer, the guy who built Shake Shack, put it like this: service is the technical delivery of a product. Hospitality is how that delivery makes the guest feel. Service is a monologue. Hospitality is a dialogue.

A monologue is telling a table what you did. A dialogue is hearing what they needed — and the bar hearing what the floor needs, the kitchen hearing what the bar needs. This week, talk to each other like the whole building is one team, because it is.
